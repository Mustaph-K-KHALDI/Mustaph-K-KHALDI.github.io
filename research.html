Research / Overview

Modeling, Control & Intelligence for Complex Dynamical Systems

My research focuses on advanced predictive control, data-driven modeling, and intelligent systems. I investigate how mathematical models, Koopman operator theory, and machine learning can improve the prediction, control, and understanding of complex nonlinear processes.

My work spans industrial chemical processes, intelligent transportation, autonomous systems, and embedded AI, connecting theoretical developments with engineering applications.

01 / Advanced Control

Koopman-Lifted Dual-Mode Predictive Control

Developed a Koopman-based dual-mode predictive control framework integrating disturbance rejection, integral action, and stability-guaranteed constrained control. Validated on an industrial-scale Fluid Catalytic Cracking (FCC) fractionator benchmark, the approach achieved faster set-point tracking, reduced steady-state errors, and improved disturbance recovery compared with linear MPC, while maintaining comparable computational efficiency.

Key Research Outcomes

Performance improvements demonstrated through comparative simulations on an industrial-scale FCC fractionator benchmark.

Lower Steady-State Offset

Average offset reduced from 0.83 to 0.18 under nominal conditions.

71%
Shorter Recovery Time

Light naphtha recovery reduced from 583 to 171 min in disturbance interval 2.

15%
Lower Disturbance Estimation Error

Average relative RMSE reduced from 0.815% to 0.689%.

21.1 ms
Average Solver Time per Iteration

Comparable to 21.2 ms for linear dual-mode MPC in simulation.

02 / Process Modeling

Computational Modeling of a Fluid Catalytic Cracking Unit

Developed a Multi-Headed Long Short-Term Memory (MH-LSTM) framework for multi-step forecasting of Fluid Catalytic Cracking (FCC) product yields. Recognizing that the reactor, regenerator, and fractionator operate on different time scales, the architecture employs dedicated LSTM heads to capture the temporal dynamics of each unit independently. Their learned representations are then integrated to enable accurate multivariate predictions. The proposed framework outperformed conventional neural network architectures in forecasting accuracy and demonstrated strong robustness under varying noise conditions.

Key Research Outcomes

Forecasting performance evaluated against conventional neural network and LSTM architectures using an FCC process simulation benchmark.

0.9962
R² Forecasting Score

Highest reported R² among the evaluated forecasting architectures.

16%
Lower Forecasting MSE

MSE reduced from 0.00439 to 0.00367 compared with CNN-LSTM.

20 min
Multi-Step Forecasting Horizon

Forecasting six FCC product streams using 60 minutes of historical data.

40 / 26 dB
Noise Robustness Evaluation

Competitive forecasting accuracy across both tested noise levels.

03 / Intelligent Mobility

Deep Koopman Learning for Highway Traffic Forecasting

Developed a Deep Learning Delay-Embedded Koopman (DL-DEK) framework for forecasting nonlinear highway traffic dynamics. The approach combines neural-network-based lifting functions with delay-embedded traffic measurements to capture spatiotemporal dependencies and enable linear prediction in a learned latent space. Evaluated on NGSIM and PeMS datasets, the framework improved forecasting accuracy over conventional Koopman methods while maintaining competitive performance with deep learning models, lower computational requirements, and interpretable traffic dynamics.

Key Research Outcomes

Forecasting performance evaluated on six NGSIM and PeMS traffic datasets against Koopman-based and deep learning benchmarks.

5–16%
Lower Forecasting Error

Reduction in average spatial MAE compared with the Hankel-DMD Koopman benchmark across six datasets.

0.5–3%
Competitive Forecasting Accuracy

Typical forecasting-error difference relative to the best-performing LSTM and CNN-LSTM models.

~4.7 s
Efficient Model Training

Typical training time across five datasets, lower than conventional LSTM and CNN-LSTM models.

KMD
Interpretable Traffic Dynamics

Identified coherent spatiotemporal patterns, congestion waves, and oscillatory traffic dynamics through Koopman Mode Decomposition.

04 / Data-Driven Modeling

From Block-Oriented Models to the Koopman Operator

Conducted a comprehensive review of block-oriented and Koopman-based approaches for nonlinear chemical process modeling, identification, and control. Complemented by a comparative benchmark of Hammerstein–Wiener, Koopman linear and bilinear, and deep neural network models on an industrial-scale FCC fractionator. The Koopman bilinear model achieved superior prediction accuracy, reduced training time, and improved robustness under measurement noise, demonstrating its potential for modeling complex nonlinear industrial processes.

Key Research Outcomes

Comparative evaluation of nonlinear modeling approaches on an industrial-scale FCC fractionator, including prediction accuracy, computational efficiency, and robustness under measurement noise.

4.07E-3
Lowest Prediction MSE

Koopman bilinear model achieved the lowest average MSE across FCC process states among the evaluated models.

57%
Lower Modeling Error

Average MSE reduced from 0.00956 to 0.00407 compared with the Hammerstein–Wiener model.

36%
Shorter Training Time

Training time reduced from 155.75 to 99.47 minutes compared with the Koopman linear model.

4 SNRs
Robustness Under Noise

Lowest mean prediction MSE and variability across 40, 26, 20, and 6 dB noise conditions.

05 / Embedded AI

YOLO for UAV and Ground-Robot Logistics

Investigated the deployment of YOLO-based object detection models for UAV and ground-robot logistics applications. Conducted a comparative evaluation of YOLOv5 and YOLOv8, examining detection accuracy, inference speed, resource utilization, and onboard versus remote processing. The study also integrated a logistics-specific image dataset and an interactive graphical interface for real-time object detection, selective visualization, and counting, providing practical insights into efficient embedded AI deployment for intelligent logistics.

Key Research Outcomes

Comparative evaluation of YOLO-based object detection, embedded inference performance, and interactive monitoring for intelligent logistics applications.

2.4×
Faster Inference

15–18 FPS
Onboard Processing Performance
